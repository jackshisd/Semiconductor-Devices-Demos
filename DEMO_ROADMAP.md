# Proposed visual demos

These are proposed additions, not implemented features. Page numbers refer to the supplied PDFs. Assign repeated slides to their latest lecture occurrence. Prioritize animations and direct manipulation; keep formulas as supporting material.

| Priority | Demo                                       | Visual interaction                                                                                                                                          | Slides                           |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1        | Combined scattering and mobility crossover | Switch phonons and ions on/off, compare electron trails, and plot both mobility limits plus Matthiessen's combined mobility against temperature and doping. | Lecture 4, 6–8                   |
| 2        | Velocity saturation                        | Turn up the field; show electron drift stops growing linearly and mark the electron/hole saturation speeds on the velocity–field graph.                     | Lecture 3, 28–31                 |
| 3        | Direct versus indirect recombination       | Animate an electron crossing an energy–momentum diagram; show why silicon requires a phonon as well as a photon.                                            | Lecture 4, 27–28                 |
| 4        | Trap-assisted generation and recombination | Watch a trap capture and release electrons/holes, connect the event sequence to minority lifetime, and switch between net generation and recombination.     | Lecture 4, 26–27; Lecture 5, 5–7 |
| 5        | Conductivity and compensated doping        | Show mobile carriers crossing a sample while donor/acceptor compensation changes carrier count; connect current, dimensions and measured resistance.        | Lecture 4, 10–15                 |
| 6        | Depletion-region R–G current               | Animate generation in reverse bias and recombination in forward bias, alongside the ideal and nonideal I–V contributions.                                   | Lecture 8, 11–14                 |
| 7        | Series resistance and high injection       | Divide voltage between a diode and its bulk resistance; show injected carrier profiles and where the ideal exponential loses validity.                      | Lecture 8, 15–16                 |

The existing phonon and impurity demos isolate each mechanism. Priority 1 adds a quantitative comparison rather than another copy of their animations. The existing diode page shows ideal injection and qualitative breakdown; priorities 6–7 would explicitly model the nonideal contributions.
