# Proposed visual demos

These are proposed additions, not implemented features. Page numbers refer to the supplied PDFs. Assign repeated slides to their latest lecture occurrence. Prioritize animations and direct manipulation; keep formulas as supporting material.

| Priority | Demo                                       | Visual interaction                                                                                                                                      | Slides                           |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| 1        | Direct versus indirect recombination       | Animate an electron crossing an energy–momentum diagram; show why silicon requires a phonon as well as a photon.                                        | Lecture 4, 27–28                 |
| 2        | Trap-assisted generation and recombination | Watch a trap capture and release electrons/holes, connect the event sequence to minority lifetime, and switch between net generation and recombination. | Lecture 4, 26–27; Lecture 5, 5–7 |
| 3        | Conductivity and compensated doping        | Show mobile carriers crossing a sample while donor/acceptor compensation changes carrier count; connect current, dimensions and measured resistance.    | Lecture 4, 10–15                 |
| 4        | Depletion-region R–G current               | Animate generation in reverse bias and recombination in forward bias, alongside the ideal and nonideal I–V contributions.                               | Lecture 8, 11–14                 |
| 5        | Series resistance and high injection       | Divide voltage between a diode and its bulk resistance; show injected carrier profiles and where the ideal exponential loses validity.                  | Lecture 8, 15–16                 |

The new Matthiessen demo combines the phonon and impurity mechanisms. The existing diode page shows ideal injection and qualitative breakdown; priorities 4–5 would explicitly model the nonideal contributions.

Implemented from this list: Matthiessen's rule (Lecture 4 slides 6–8) and velocity saturation (Lecture 3 slide 28). Thermal-motion/drift and momentum/free-flight demos also appear on slides 23–25 and 29–32.
